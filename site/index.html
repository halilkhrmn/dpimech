What is it?

Some internet providers block sites and apps with DPI (deep packet inspection). Well-known open-source tools such as zapret, ByeDPI and GoodbyeDPI get around it, but they are command-line programs with lots of options. DPIMech installs them for you, finds settings that work on your connection and runs them in the background.

Not a VPN. Your traffic still goes straight to the sites. Nothing is sent through another server, so it stays fast, and nobody in the middle sees what you do.

  • Setup wizard: pick what you want to open (Discord, YouTube, ...), it tests and saves a working setup.
  • Only for chosen apps, or for the whole computer.
  • Strategy Lab: tries many settings against real sites and ranks them.
  • Tray icon, notifications, start with the computer.
  • Small and native: no browser engine inside.
  • English, Türkçe, Русский.

Which system can do what?

WindowsPer app (ByeDPI + ProxiFyre) or whole computer (zapret, GoodbyeDPI).
LinuxPer app or whole computer (zapret nfqws / tpws, ByeDPI) with systemd and nftables.
macOSLocal proxy (tpws, ByeDPI) for apps and browsers you point at it.

Is it safe?

  • All code is open. Engines are downloaded from their official GitHub releases and checked against pinned SHA-256 hashes.
  • The background service only starts programs from its own protected folder and checks every engine option against an allowlist.
  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking.
  • Antivirus programs sometimes flag DPI tools because they work on network packets. The app can add an exclusion for its engine folder if you ask it to.
